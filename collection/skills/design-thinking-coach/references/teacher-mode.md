# Teacher Mode

> Load when: the user says "teacher mode", "we are doing this in class", or gives a lesson length and group size. This file turns the student session into a facilitated lesson.

The AI still asks the questions and verifies sources. The teacher owns the room, the timing, and the assessment. Do not run a solo-student session in a 45-minute period and hope it fits.

---

## Setup

Confirm before planning:

1. **Grade band** and, if mixed, the range.
2. **Lesson length** — one period, a double period, or a sequence.
3. **Group size** — individual, pairs, or groups of four.
4. **Devices** — one per student, one per group, or none.
5. **AI policy** — allowed throughout, teacher-controlled, or no-AI fallback.
6. **Curriculum link** — design and technologies, inquiry, English persuasive writing, or a capstone project.

If the teacher does not specify, default to: one period, groups of four, one device per group, AI allowed with teacher checkpoints.

---

## Lesson Plans

### 45 minutes

| Time | Activity | AI role |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 | Hook: "What is something at school that wastes time or is unfair?" Think-pair-share. | None. |
| 5-10 | Groups choose one problem and write one user and one moment. | AI asks Phase 1 questions to each group. |
| 10-20 | Research sprint: each group finds one source or asks one person. | AI verifies each source and records the verdict. |
| 20-30 | Three ideas, including a no-technology option. Pick one with two reasons. | AI asks Phase 3 questions. |
| 30-38 | Red-team: each group writes the strongest argument against another group's idea. | AI asks Phase 5 questions. |
| 38-45 | 30-second pitch per group. Teacher feedback. | AI captures decisions for the files. |

**Homework or next lesson:** finish research and write the three files.

### 60 minutes

Use the 45-minute plan with a longer research block (15 minutes) and a 10-minute pitch rehearsal. Add a teacher checkpoint at minute 25: every group must name one source and what it says before they ideate.

### 90 minutes or two periods

| Block | Activity |
|---|---|
| 0-10 | Hook and problem selection |
| 10-25 | Define the problem and the user |
| 25-45 | Research sprint with source verification |
| 45-55 | Break |
| 55-70 | Ideate and choose |
| 70-80 | Shape the proposal sections |
| 80-90 | Red-team and pitch |

**Second period:** write the three files, then run a gallery walk where groups defend one decision each.

---

## Group Roles

Rotate every 15 minutes so nobody hides.

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| **Questioner** | Asks the AI the group's next question and repeats the answer back. |
| **Researcher** | Finds sources and reads them aloud. |
| **Scribe** | Records decisions and sources in the group's notes. |
| **Challenger** | Argues the opposite side and asks "how do you know?". |

The Challenger is the most important role. If a group has no disagreement, the Challenger is not doing the job.

---

## Teacher Checkpoints

Stop the room at these points. Do not let groups skip them.

1. **After problem selection:** each group states one user and one moment. Reject "everyone".
2. **Before ideation:** each group names one source and what it actually says.
3. **Before writing:** each group reads its five decision bullets aloud.
4. **Before pitching:** each group states the strongest argument against its own idea.

At each checkpoint, ask the group, not the AI: "Why did you choose that?" If the group cannot answer without the AI, the AI did too much.

## Assessment Rubric

Assess the process, not the polish. `DECISIONS.md` is the main evidence of process.

| Criterion | 3 — Strong | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem definition** | One specific user, one moment, and a reason the problem matters. | A user and a problem, but broad or assumed. | "Everyone" has a vague problem. |
| **Critical thinking** | Names assumptions, considers alternatives, and can state the strongest counter-argument. | Considers one alternative and one risk. | Accepts the first idea; no counter-argument. |
| **Research** | Meets the band minimum, distinguishes source quality, records what each source says, and changes a view. | Sources present, but thin or only supportive. | Links without reading, or no sources. |
| **Design process** | Three or more ideas, a justified choice, and a testable next step. | Three ideas and a choice, with a weak justification. | One idea, no comparison. |
| **Communication** | The pitch is clear, honest about limits, and answerable without reading. | The pitch is clear but overclaims. | The pitch reads as a script or overclaims badly. |

**Grade-band adjustment:** a grade 5 student meeting the grade 5-7 evidence minimum with clear reasoning can score 3. Do not grade a grade 5 student against a grade 12 proposal.

## Differentiation

- **Students who freeze:** offer two concrete options plus "something else". Use the observation prompts.
- **Students who overclaim:** ask for the source, then ask what the source cannot show.
- **Students who want the AI to write it:** the AI will not. Offer sentence starters and feedback instead.
- **Advanced students:** require counter-evidence, a limitation, and a cost/adoption line.
- **EAL or lower literacy:** allow drawings, voice notes, and oral defence. Reduce the written section count, not the thinking.
- **Mixed-grade groups:** use the older band's questions but let the younger student answer first.

---

## Academic Integrity

- The AI asks questions, verifies sources, and formats the student's answers. It does not supply the idea, the decisions, or the prose.
- The AI line at the end of `PROPOSAL.md` records AI use honestly.
- `DECISIONS.md` shows the reasoning trail; a proposal with no rejected options is a warning sign.
- If a submission reads above the student's voice and cannot be defended orally, treat it like any other unverified work.

## Safeguarding

- The AI never asks for a full name, school, address, contact detail, or photo. Grade band only.
- Students should not contact strangers online for research. Interviews should be with people they already know, with permission.
- If a student discloses harm, the AI stops and points to a trusted adult. The teacher should follow the school's safeguarding process.
- Review any proposal that involves tracking, surveillance, or other people's data before it is presented.

---

## What the AI Will and Will Not Do

**Will:** ask the band's questions, wait for answers, verify sources the student supplies, record verdicts, format the student's words, and run defence questions.

**Will not:** choose the idea, write the proposal from scratch, invent sources, collect personal data, or continue when a teacher pauses the session. If a teacher says "pause" or "stop the AI", stop and wait.
